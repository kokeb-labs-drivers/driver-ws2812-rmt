Timing¶

How the driver turns a datasheet into a waveform, and which variant to pick for your LEDs.

All of them, with the driver API for each: Datasheet map.

The bit¶

A bit is a high pulse followed by a low one, and the length of the high pulse is the value: a short high is 0, a long high is 1. The data line idles low. After the last bit of the frame the line stays low for the reset time, which latches the colours.

Symbol Meaning
T0H, T0L 0 code: high time, low time
T1H, T1L 1 code: high time, low time
RES Low time, above which the pixels latch and the next bit starts a new frame

Each datasheet gives a window (a minimum and a maximum) for each of the four times, sometimes a bit period, and a minimum for RES. The windows are not the same across parts or even across revisions of one part. The WS2812B has four generations (2013, Mar-2017, V5, V6) whose windows have no waveform in common: the T1L of the Mar-2017 sheet is 220 to 420 ns, and the T1L of V5 is 580 ns to 1 µs.

Pick the variant of your LEDs¶

Config::variant selects one set of windows. Each named variant is the intersection of the windows of every document of that variant, so a waveform inside it satisfies all of them.

Variant Parts Reset above Documents (id of the map)
Ws2812bV5 (default) WS2812B V5 and V6, WS2812B-2020, WS2812C-2020 280 µs b-v5, b-v5-w, b-v6-h, b-v6, b-2020, c-2020, b-1313
Ws2812bPreV5 WS2812B of 2013 to 2017, WS2812S 300 µs; low inside a frame below 50 µs b-af, b-cn, b-mar17, ws2812s
Ws2812 WS2812 (6 pin) 50 µs ws2812
Ws2812c WS2812C (5050), WS2812-2020 280 µs c-a, c-b, ws-2020
Ws2812d WS2812D-F8 280 µs d-f8
Sk6812 SK6812 (RGB) 80 µs; low inside a frame below 50 µs sk-01
Sk6812Rgbw SK6812RGBW 80 µs; low inside a frame below 20 µs skw-01, skw-03, skw-07
Sk6812Mini SK6812MINI, MINI-E 80 µs; low inside a frame below 20 µs skm-e; skm (windows only)
Custom any other part with this frame your value yours

How to tell a WS2812B generation: the V5 and V6 sheets say "Ver. No." and show a reset of 280 µs, T1H and T1L of 580 ns to 1 µs; older sheets show 300 ± 150 ns style values and a reset of 50 or 300 µs. The date of the datasheet you bought the strip with, or the silkscreen revision, is the guide. A strip that flickers or shows wrong colours with the default variant usually wants Ws2812bPreV5. WaveformInWindow(variant, wave) checks one waveform against one variant's windows: mixed chains.

The Ws2812bPreV5 bit period is limited to 950 to 1550 ns, not the 650 to 1850 ns of the 2013 sheets: the WS2812S sheet prints 1.25 µs +-300 ns and its documents are part of the variant. The profile is the intersection, so the tighter period applies.

The windows of every document are in the model of the tests, tests/mock_ws2812_rmt.hpp (kDocuments), which is written from the datasheets and kept apart from the driver's profile table, so that a transcription error in either shows up as a test failure.

How a waveform is resolved¶

EnsureInitialized() asks the port for its real tick rate and calls ResolveWaveform():

timing.cpp
// What the driver does at start-up, by hand: nanoseconds into ticks at the port's real rate.
bool WaveformFor20Mhz() {
  const auto wave = ws2812_rmt::ResolveWaveform(
      ws2812_rmt::ProfileTiming(ws2812_rmt::Variant::Ws2812bV5), 20'000'000, 0);
  if (!wave) {
    return false; // TimingUnreachable
  }
  // A 20 MHz tick is 50 ns: T0H 7 ticks (350 ns), T0L 19, T1H 14, T1L 12, reset 6000 ticks (300
  // us).
  return (wave.value.t0h_ticks == 7U) && (wave.value.reset_ticks == 6000U);
}
  1. Each duration is converted to ticks: the smallest tick count at or above the window minimum, the largest at or below the maximum. The edges are inclusive. The aim is the nominal value of the profile, clamped to the window.
  2. If no tick count fits a window (the tick is longer than the window), the result is TimingUnreachable, and nothing is sent. The driver also refuses a named variant at a rate below kMinResolutionHz (20 MHz): see Why 20 MHz.
  3. A period minimum (the V6 data cycle of 1250 ns, the SK6812 code period of 1200 ns) is met by lengthening the low of a bit, which stays inside its window.
  4. The reset is strictly above the minimum: Config::reset_us, or the datasheet minimum plus 20 µs when it is 0. The reset is a timed low after the last bit: the ESP32 port waits it with the line idle low (eot_level), so no RMT symbol has to hold it and the 32 767-tick half-symbol limit does not apply to it.
  5. The longest low inside a frame, max_gap_ticks, is the smallest latch time any document of the variant prints, less 10 percent, and strictly below that: 252 µs for V5 (latch above 280 µs), 45 µs for the 2013 WS2812B and the SK6812 (their sheets print a 50 µs latch next to the larger reset the driver sends), 18 µs for SK6812RGBW and MINI (the sheet says the low must stay below 20 µs). The port must not leave a gap that long inside a frame, or the pixels latch half a frame. The reset the driver sends is the larger value, which every generation accepts.

The V5 nominals at 20 MHz are 7 ticks for T0H, 19 for T0L, and 13 each for T1H and T1L (350, 950, 650 and 650 ns). The tick rate must be the real one: the port reports what the peripheral runs at, and the driver converts at that rate.

Why V5 aims T0H at 350 ns¶

The V5 window of T0H is 220 to 380 ns (the sheets print windows, no typical value; its centre is 300 ns), but the V6 sheet adds a data cycle of at least 1250 ns (T0H + T0L and T1H + T1L). The Ws2812bV5 profile covers V6, so the cycle applies. With T0H 300 ns and T0L 950 ns (19 ticks of 50 ns) the cycle is exactly 1250 ns: any clock error below nominal shortens it under the limit (at +1000 ppm 1248.75 ns). With T0H 350 ns (7 ticks) the cycle is 1300 ns, 4 % above the minimum, while T0H keeps 30 ns of margin below its 380 ns maximum and T0L 50 ns below its 1000 ns maximum. 350 and 950 ns are the best pair for the 0 code at 20 MHz: no other tick pair beats 30 ns of margin on every edge. The 1 code is 650 + 650 = 1300 ns as well, 70 ns from both window edges, so every bit takes 1300 ns and a strip of 1024 pixels still meets the 30 frames per second of the sheets. The test A tick-rate error of +-1000 ppm stays inside every window fails with 300 ns.

Thin margins that remain at 20 MHz, against the printed windows: 30 ns on the V5 profile (T0H; the 1300 ns bit is 50 ns above the 1250 ns cycle), 50 ns on the 2013 to 2017 WS2812B, SK6812RGBW and SK6812MINI profiles, 70 ns on WS2812C and 80 ns on WS2812D. A clock within 1000 ppm moves an edge by about 1 ns, so none of them leaves its window.

Why 20 MHz¶

At 10 MHz a tick is 100 ns. The T0H window of V5 (220 to 380 ns) then holds a single tick count (300 ns), and a bit period of 1250 ns can only be met with T0L at the very edge of its window (1000 ns): the margin is 0 ns, and a clock error of 1000 ppm leaves the window (the same holds at 8, 9.6 and 12 MHz). At 16 MHz V5 still sits exactly on an edge, and the rates between 16 and 20 MHz leave no margin to spare either. At 20 MHz (50 ns ticks) every window holds several tick counts, so the aimed values sit 30 ns or more from the edges and a clock error of 1000 ppm cannot push them out. kRecommendedResolutionHz and kMinResolutionHz are both 20 MHz (a finer rate such as 40 MHz or 80 MHz keeps the margins), and the driver returns TimingUnreachable below it for a named variant (Custom is not limited: its windows are yours).

Frame time¶

The time of a frame is pixels x bits per pixel x the longer bit period, plus the reset. FrameTimeNs(), MaxRefreshHz() and MaxPixelsAtRate() compute it from a waveform:

timing.cpp
// How many pixels fit in a 30 Hz refresh, and what a 60-pixel frame costs.
void FrameBudget() {
  const auto wave = ws2812_rmt::ResolveWaveform(
      ws2812_rmt::ProfileTiming(ws2812_rmt::Variant::Ws2812bV5), 20'000'000, 0);
  const size_t longest = ws2812_rmt::MaxPixelsAtRate(30, 3, wave.value); // 1058
  const uint64_t frame_ns = ws2812_rmt::FrameTimeNs(60, 3, wave.value);  // 2 172 000 ns
  const uint32_t level_mv =
      ws2812_rmt::MinLogicHighMv(ws2812_rmt::Variant::Ws2812bV5, 5000); // 2750
  static_cast<void>(longest);
  static_cast<void>(frame_ns);
  static_cast<void>(level_mv);
}

A 60-pixel WS2812B strip takes about 2.2 ms per frame, and a 1024-pixel strip about 32 ms (the datasheets promise at least 1024 pixels at 30 frames per second). A frame is sent in one gapless stream and the call blocks until it and the reset are out; the reference ESP32 port runs the stream without the CPU (RMT, with DMA where the part has it) and then spends the reset (300 µs) in a busy wait of the CPU. The port must not underrun: see Porting.

Mixed chains¶

Pixels of two WS2812B generations in one chain need one waveform inside both windows, and for the 2013 and V5 generations there is none. WaveformInWindow() tells which variants accept a waveform:

timing.cpp
// Two generations in one chain: is one waveform legal for both?
bool OneWaveformServesBoth() {
  const auto wave = ws2812_rmt::ResolveWaveform(
      ws2812_rmt::ProfileTiming(ws2812_rmt::Variant::Ws2812bV5), 20'000'000, 0);
  return wave && ws2812_rmt::WaveformInWindow(ws2812_rmt::Variant::Ws2812bV5, wave.value) &&
         ws2812_rmt::WaveformInWindow(ws2812_rmt::Variant::Ws2812bPreV5,
                                      wave.value); // false: use Custom
}

When it returns false for a pair you must have, measure which waveform both strips accept and declare it with Custom (Configuration), or split the strip into two outputs.

What is not covered¶

  • WS2812E is not named: no datasheet could be obtained for it. Reach it through Custom once you hold its sheet.
  • WS2812D-F5 is the same family as the F8 and unverified; the current WS2812B V7 sheet was not obtained.
  • The original WS2812 lists a low-speed 400 kbit/s mode with no windows. It is reachable through Custom.
  • WS2811 and other drivers with a 400 kbit/s mode, dual-signal parts (a backup data line), SPI-clocked pixels and 16-bit-per-channel parts use a different frame or wire.
  • No datasheet gives the pixel's power-on reset time or an upper bound for the reset: both are assumptions with a knob (Limitations).
